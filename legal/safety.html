Safety Center

Last updated 22 November 2026

Puddleton should feel like a cosy game night with friends. Here is how we keep it that way, and what you can do if something feels wrong.

If someone is in danger right now, call your local emergency number (999 in the UK, 911 in the US, 112 in the EU).

For players

  • Lying in the game is fine. Being cruel is not. Accuse the baker, bluff about your role, but leave real people out of it.
  • Keep real life private. Never share your real name, address, school, phone number, social media or photos, and don't ask anyone for theirs.
  • Stay in the game. If someone you met in a match asks you to move to another app, to send pictures, or to keep a secret from your parents, that is a red flag. Don't reply: block, report, and tell an adult you trust.
  • You never have to explain why. If someone makes you uncomfortable, mute or block them.
  • Take breaks. Losing streaks happen to every detective. Step away, get a snack, come back later.

For parents and carers

Puddleton is a social deduction game: players are secretly given roles (such as the Mafia or the detective) and talk, bluff and vote to find each other out. "Eliminations" are cartoon and bloodless. Players are guests: there are no accounts, no payments and no ads.

When your child starts, they choose an age band. Settings follow it:

  • Play a round together. It is the quickest way to see how chat, voting and the tools work.
  • Talk about red flags: requests for personal details, pictures, gifts, secrets, or moving to another app.
  • Your rights: you can ask us to see or delete what we keep about your child. Email sidsvictus@gmail.com; we answer within one month. Details are in our privacy policy.

Mute, block and report

Tap a player's bean or name during a game to open their options.

Mute
Hides their chat from you. They are not told. Use it when someone is just annoying.
Block
Hides them and their messages from you and stops them whispering to you. They are not told.
Report
Sends the report to us with a snapshot of the match so far: public chat, whispers, votes and actions. Pick a reason and, if you like, add a short note. The player is not told who reported them.

You can mute or block and report the same player. If you can't report in the game, email sidsvictus@gmail.com with the room, roughly when it happened, the player's display name and what they did.

What happens after a report

  1. We receive the report together with the match log snapshot, so we see what was actually said and done, not just one side.
  2. A person at After Dark reviews it against our code of conduct. Reports about children's safety go first.
  3. If a rule was broken, we act: a warning, a name reset, removing content, a chat mute, or a temporary or permanent ban.
  4. The player we act against gets a statement of reasons: what we did, which rule, and how to appeal.
  5. If you reported by email, we reply with what we decided. To protect everyone's privacy we may not share every detail.

Reports and their snapshots are kept for one year, then deleted. Match logs that were never reported are deleted after 30 days.

How to appeal

If you were muted or banned and think we got it wrong, email sidsvictus@gmail.com with "Appeal" in the subject, your display name, roughly when it happened, and why you think the decision was wrong. You can appeal for up to six months.

A person reviews the decision again with the full report and match log, and we aim to reply within 14 days. If we were wrong, we lift the sanction and clear it from your record. More detail is in the terms.

Illegal content

Some things are crimes, not just rule breaks. We report illegal content to the authorities. That includes child sexual abuse material and attempts to groom or exploit children, which we report to the US National Center for Missing and Exploited Children and any other authority where the law requires. If we believe someone's life or safety is at risk, we contact the police. We keep the evidence securely for as long as the law requires.
