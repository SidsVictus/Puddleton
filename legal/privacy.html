Privacy

Last updated 22 November 2026

Puddleton is a game, so we only keep what the game needs to run and to keep players safe. We do not sell your data, we show no ads, and nothing on the island tracks you around the web.

For kids

  • We never ask for your real name, your email, your address or your school. Please don't tell anyone in a game, either.
  • If you are under 13, you chat with ready-made phrases, so nobody can ask you for personal things. You can't whisper and you can't add friends.
  • The game remembers you with a random code saved on your device. It is not your name. You or a grown-up can wipe it whenever you like.
  • We never sell anything about you, and there are no ads following you around.
  • If someone is mean or makes you feel weird, use Mute, Block or Report, and tell a grown-up you trust.
  • Grown-ups can email us at sidsvictus@gmail.com to see or delete what we keep.

1Who we are

Puddleton is made by After Dark, an independent game studio run by C. Siddarth Ram in Hyderabad, Telangana, India. After Dark decides how and why the personal data described here is used, which makes us the "controller" under data protection law.

Contact for anything about privacy: sidsvictus@gmail.com. We read every message.

Right now you play as a guest: there are no accounts and nothing is for sale. If we add accounts (Google, Discord or an email link) or purchases, we will update this page first and tell you in the game.

2What we collect

Here is everything the game handles, in plain words.

Guest id and secret
Random codes your device makes the first time you play, so the server knows you are the same player next time. They are not linked to your real identity.
Age band
Under 13, 13 to 17, or 18 and over, which you choose yourself. We use it to pick your chat and safety settings. We never ask for your birthday.
Display name
The name other players see in a game. Please don't use your real full name.
Outfits and settings
What your bean wears, your house and effects, and settings such as sound.
Game stats
How many games you played and how many you won with each role.
Match logs
A record of each match: public chat, whispers, votes and night actions. We keep it so we can check reports fairly.
Reports
When a player reports someone, we save the report (who reported whom, the reason and any note) together with a snapshot of that match's log.
Sanctions
Warnings, mutes and bans we apply, with the reason and any appeal.
IP address
Your device's IP address reaches our server because that is how the internet delivers the game to you. The game server does not store it and does not use it for bans.
Device token
A random token saved on your device that keeps your guest session going and stops banned players from coming straight back.
Bot check
Cloudflare Turnstile checks that a real person is starting a game, using technical signals from your browser such as your IP address and browser details. We only receive a pass or a fail.
Page counts
We count visits to our pages with GoatCounter, which uses no cookies and does not store IP addresses. We only see totals, such as how many people opened a page.
Messages you send us
If you email us, we get your email address and whatever you write.

What we do not collect: your real name, email (unless you write to us), phone number, precise location, contacts, photos, voice, payment details or advertising ids.

3Why we use it, and our legal bases

Data protection law (the GDPR in the EU and the UK GDPR) asks us to name a legal reason for each use. Ours are:

To run the game you asked to play
Guest id, device token, age band, display name, outfits, settings, stats and the live game. Legal basis: performing our agreement with you (the terms).
To keep players safe and the game fair
Match logs, reports, sanctions, chat filters, the device token and the bot check. Legal basis: our legitimate interest, and yours, in a safe and fair game. We keep this data for short, fixed times and only look at match logs when something is reported or we must investigate abuse.
To count visits
Totals from GoatCounter that tell us whether people find the game. Legal basis: legitimate interest. No one can be identified from these counts.
To follow the law
Reporting illegal content such as child sexual abuse material to the authorities, keeping it for as long as the law requires, and answering lawful requests. Legal basis: legal obligation.
To answer you
Replying to your emails and requests. Legal basis: legitimate interest, or a legal obligation when you use your rights.

We do not use your data for advertising, we do not build profiles to target you, and we make no automated decisions with legal or similarly serious effects. Chat filters work automatically, but sanctions are decided or checked by a person.

4Children

Puddleton is for everyone, including kids, so it is a mixed audience game. When you start, we ask for your age band and set the game up to match. Every player under 18 gets high privacy settings by default.

Under 13

  • No personal information asked for. We don't ask for a real name, email or any way to contact the child. Kids pick their own display name; we ask them to use a nickname, and names with an email address or phone number are refused. The few things we keep (the random guest id, display name, device token, stats and match logs) are used only to run the game and keep it safe, never to contact, profile or advertise to a child.
  • Phrase chat only. Players pick from ready-made phrases, so personal details cannot be typed into chat.
  • No whispers and no friend links. There is no way to connect with someone outside a game.

13 to 17

  • Typed chat goes through filters. Whispers are allowed, but the whole table sees that a whisper happened (not what it said).
  • Chat with strangers outside these settings is not available.

18 and over

  • Filtered typed chat, plus chat with strangers if the player chooses to turn it on. It is off until they do.

Parents and carers

You can ask us to show you, or delete, what we keep about your child. Email sidsvictus@gmail.com with your child's display name and, if you can, roughly when they played. Because we do not know who our players are, we may ask a couple of questions to make sure we find the right data. We answer within one month. If we learn that we hold personal information from a child under 13 that we should not have, we delete it.

Age is self-declared. If we have reason to think a player is younger than the band they chose, we apply the younger settings.

5Who helps us run it

A few companies process data for us, under contracts that only let them use it to provide their service to us:

Cloudflare
Cloudflare, Inc. (United States) runs the game server, stores game data and provides the Turnstile bot check. Cloudflare has data centres around the world, so data may be handled in many countries.
Surge
Surge (Chloi Inc.) hosts the website files. Like any web host, it keeps short-lived request logs, including IP addresses, to fix problems and block abuse.
GoatCounter
GoatCounter counts page visits without cookies and without storing IP addresses. It is operated from Ireland and stores its data in Finland and Germany.
Google (Gmail)
Only if you email us: our inbox is hosted by Google.

We share data with the police or other authorities only when the law requires it, for example to report child sexual abuse material or a threat to someone's life. We never sell personal data or share it for advertising.

7Your rights

Wherever you live, you can ask us to:

  • Access the data we hold about you.
  • Export it in a common, machine-readable format.
  • Delete it.
  • Correct anything that is wrong.
  • Object to how we use it, or ask us to pause using it.

Email sidsvictus@gmail.com. Because you play as a guest, tell us your display name and roughly when you played so we can find you; we may ask a question to check the data is yours. It is free, and we answer within one month (if a request is unusually complex we may need up to two more months, and we will tell you why). You can also clear your guest data yourself in the game's settings, which deletes it from our server too. Clearing this site's data in your browser only removes the copy on your device; the server copy is then deleted after 12 months without play, or straight away if you email us.

We do not sell personal information or share it for cross-context behavioural advertising, as those terms are used in US state privacy laws.

8Cookies and local storage

Puddleton sets no advertising or tracking cookies. To work, the game saves a few things in your browser's local storage: your guest id and secret, device token, age band, display name, outfit, settings, whether you have seen the how-to-play guide, and your seat in the game you are in. It may also cache game files so the island loads faster.

All of this is strictly necessary to give you the game you asked for, so the law does not require a consent banner. GoatCounter stores nothing in your browser. Cloudflare's Turnstile bot check may use strictly necessary signals to tell people from bots.

9International transfers

Our helpers run servers in several countries, including the United States. When personal data leaves the UK or the European Economic Area, we rely on the safeguards the law provides: adequacy decisions (such as the EU-US Data Privacy Framework and its UK extension, for certified companies) or the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses with the UK addendum.

10Keeping it safe

Connections are encrypted (HTTPS), the game server does not store IP addresses, your guest secret stays on your device, and only the owner of After Dark can open the admin tools. No system is perfect: if something goes wrong that puts your data at risk, we will act quickly and tell you and the authorities when the law requires.

11Changes

When we change this page we update the date at the top. If a change matters, for example new kinds of data or a new helper, we will tell players in the game before it takes effect.

12Contact and complaints

Questions or worries: sidsvictus@gmail.com. If you are not happy with our answer, you can complain to a data protection authority: in the UK, the Information Commissioner's Office; in the EU, the authority where you live. We would be grateful for the chance to fix things first.
